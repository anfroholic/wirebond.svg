Wire Bond Bench · guide · November 2026

Files in and out

Which files Wire Bond Bench and wbx.py can read, what each one gives them, how much of a bonded design to put in your KiCad board, and what comes back out.

The short version
  • Two things go in: a substrate and a die. The substrate is a KiCad footprint or board with the bond lands. The die is a GDS, or a footprint if you don't have the GDS yet.
  • KiCad files can carry as much as you like. A bare padring is enough. Nets, sketched lines or full wire footprints let the app pair pads with lands for you.
  • Most of what comes out is for KiCad: the assembled board with the die and wires in 3D as one STEP, a die footprint, or a die kit with symbol and STEP model. The bondlist and .wb.svg are for the bonding house.
  • An exported board reads back in, and so does a .wb.svg. Load either and the die, wires and wire types return. wbx.py reads the .wb.svg too.
  • The 3D is one STEP file. It holds the die and every wire, and it is embedded in the board, so there's no folder of models to keep with it. Section 6 has how that was decided and what is still open.

1The map

There are two tools. Wire Bond Bench is the browser app in app/. It reads design files, pairs pads with lands and writes KiCad files. wbx.py is a small Python tool that checks a .wb.svg and turns it into files a bonder can use. Click any file to see what it carries.

2What each file brings in

Drop files anywhere on the app, or on the Substrate and Die boxes on the left. A .kicad_pcb becomes the substrate. A GDS becomes the die. Drop two footprints together and the smaller one becomes the die. A single footprint becomes the substrate, unless you already have one or it says it's a die (wb_kind = die).

GDS .gds .gds2 .gdsii .gds.gz

The die as it was taped out. This is the best source, because it holds the real pad openings and the artwork.

Die sizePad openingsPad numbersNet namesArtworkThickness
How it's read
  • Size comes from the top cell's bounding box.
  • Pads. Bond pads are nearly all one size and sit around the die edge, so the app ranks each layer by how regular its edge boxes are. You can pick a different layer, set size limits, drop internal shapes, or remove single pads.
  • Net names come from text labels over each pad, on any layer. A label like 12:clk gives pad number 12 and net clk.
  • Pin 1. Labels with numbers set the numbering. Without them, it starts from the corner with the QR code cell and goes round the die. You can override both.
  • Artwork is drawn from the metal layers. It goes into the KiCad footprint image, and onto the die in the STEP model in two colours, the die's and one accent.
Not read
  • Die thickness and attach height, which a GDS doesn't hold. The app assumes them, and you can change them under Placement. OASIS isn't read, so export the die as GDS first.

KiCad footprint, as substrate .kicad_mod

A padring footprint, like wafer.space's COB padrings. It's the quickest way in when the board isn't drawn yet.

LandsLand netsPaddleSketched wires
How it's read
  • Lands are the numbered SMD pads on front copper. A pad marked as a fiducial is never a land.
  • The paddle is the biggest pad, when it's too long to be a land. The die is centred on it.
  • Nets on the lands make the Net names regime work.
  • Lines on Cmts.User inside the footprint are read as wire sketches for the Drawn lines regime.

KiCad footprint, as die .kicad_mod

For when you don't have the GDS yet, or only have a vendor's pad table. examples/waferspace/waferspace_1x1_die.kicad_mod is one.

Die sizePadsPad numbersNet namesArtwork
How it's read
  • Pads are the footprint's numbered pads. A pad's net name is its pin function, or else its net.
  • The app's own die footprint has no copper pads. It has filled F.Fab rectangles with number net text over each one. Those are read as pads too, so an exported die comes back.
  • Outline is the largest unfilled rectangle on F.Fab, F.CrtYd, Edge.Cuts or Dwgs.User. Without one, it's the extent of the Fab and courtyard lines, or the pads plus 0.1 mm as a last resort.

KiCad board .kicad_pcb

Your real board. The app works on one footprint in it, the substrate, and leaves the rest of the board as it is.

LandsLand netsDieWiresWire types
How it's read
  • Substrate. The app suggests the footprint with the most front-copper SMD pads, and prefers one whose library name says padring, COB, bond or substrate. You can pick another, or use the whole board.
  • Die. Any footprint with wb_kind = die comes in as a die, at its position and rotation. It also brings wb_thickness_um and wb_attach_um if set.
  • Wires. Footprints with wb_kind = wire feed the From the board regime.
  • Wire types come from the WBLIB footprint's wb_type_<name> fields, or from the older wb_profile_<i>_… board properties.

Project files .kicad_pro .kicad_sch

They aren't read for data. They're carried, unchanged, into the exported board zip.

Design rulesSchematic
  • KiCad keeps design rules in the .kicad_pro. A board copied without it gets KiCad's defaults. On the 1x0p5 COB that meant 1381 DRC items instead of 22.
  • Drop them with the board or afterwards. The app says when they're loaded.

DRC rules .json

The limits the Checks tab measures against: wire gap, edge clearance, longest wire and the rest. Each rule says where its number comes from.

Rule valuesOn or off
  • Open DRC rules in the top bar, then Import JSON. Export JSON writes wbx_drc_rules.json, so a bonding house's numbers can be shared and reused.

Bond diagram .wb.svg

The wirebond.svg format from SPEC.md. It's a true-scale drawing that is also the data. The app opens one whole, its own or one from wbx.py, as a session to change and export again. wbx.py reads it too.

DiePadsLandsWiresLoop profilesLayer stackFiducialsTolerance
  • python tools/wbx.py check file.wb.svg validates it. place finds how far the die can move or turn before a check fails. export writes CSV and DXF.
  • wbx.py reads one die at 0°. The app writes turned and stacked die correctly, but wbx.py can't read them yet.

3How much to put in KiCad

You don't have to adopt a convention to use the app. Each level below is something you can add to the substrate in KiCad, and each one gives the app more to work from. Pick a level to see what to add and what it gives you.

Whatever the level, keep the .kicad_pro with the board. It holds the design rules. Level 3 is rarely written by hand. You export it from the app once, and after that it reads back in.

4What comes out

Everything in the app is under Put it in KiCad in the top bar. Files are written in the browser and downloaded. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Assembled board, with 3D

.zip · needs a substrate

Your board with the die and every wire added. Everything else on the board is unchanged, byte for byte.

<board>_bonded.zip
├─ <board>_bonded.kicad_pcb
├─ <board>_bonded.kicad_pro  if loaded
├─ <board>_bonded.kicad_sch  if loaded
└─ <board>_bond.step         copy of the embedded STEP

The 3D is one STEP: the die, its attach layer, its pads and every wire as named, coloured solids. The die's top face is split into faces of two colours traced from its artwork, so you see your chip on the board. It is embedded in the board on the first die's footprint, and KiCad's own STEP export carries it on. The loose copy is for mechanical CAD. Change anything and export again; the new STEP replaces the old one in the board.

Use it to see the bonded assembly in KiCad's 3D viewer, hand the board's STEP to mechanical CAD with the wires in it, and keep the wiring in version control with the board.

The die alone, for a footprint library. It has the outline on Fab and courtyard, a filled Fab rectangle with number net text for each pad, and the artwork as an image. It has no copper.

Bond diagram

A true-scale drawing of the bond area that any browser opens, with hover text on every wire, pad and land. It has a plan view, a side view of the layer stack, and the loop profiles.

From wbx.py

Files for offline programming of a bonder, all centred on the die.

The two bondlists have different columns. The app's has type, length_um and peak_um. wbx.py's has tier and profile, because it reads them from the .wb.svg. Both use µm from the die centre, with y up.

5Inside the exported board

KiCad has no bond wire object. The export uses ordinary footprints and hidden wb_ fields instead, which KiCad keeps and lets you edit without understanding them. This is one wire as the app writes it, with the UUIDs and font settings left out.

(footprint "wbx_wire"
1  (layer "F.Cu") (at 104.66 98.2)
  (property "Reference" "W01" (hide yes))
  (property "Value" "STD25" (hide yes))
2  (property "wb_kind" "wire")
3  (property "wb_from" "D1.1")
  (property "wb_to"   "L.1")
4  (property "wb_type" "STD25")
  (property "wb_net"  "clk")
  (property "wb_seq"  "1")
5  (attr board_only exclude_from_pos_files
        exclude_from_bom allow_missing_courtyard)
6  (fp_line (start 0 0) (end -1.62 -0.44)
    (stroke (width 0.025) (type solid))
    (layer "User.1")))
  1. The footprint sits at the first bond, on the die pad, in board mm.
  2. wb_kind is how a reader finds the app's footprints: die, wire or type_library. Anything without it is your board.
  3. wb_from is <die>.<pad>. wb_to is the land, as <ref>.<pad>. A wire to another die names its pad and adds wb_to_kind = die.
  4. wb_type names an entry in the WBLIB footprint, for example wb_type_STD25 = ball-wedge Au 25 forward 180/0 30/12% -20/35%: style, material, diameter in µm, direction and loop moves. GENERIC and UNDEFINED are reserved.
  5. Wires take no board space, so they're left out of the BOM and position files and don't need a courtyard. Without that, boards that require courtyards would flag every wire.
  6. The line runs to the second bond on the first free User.1 to User.9 layer, shown as Wirebond. Its width is the wire diameter. A generic or undefined wire's line has width zero, solid or dashed.

A wire footprint has no 3D model of its own. Its tube is in the one STEP on the die footprint, named W01 like the footprint.

What each footprint carries

Footprintwb_kindDrawnFields
Die, named D1, D2…dieOutline on F.Fab and F.CrtYd, filled Fab pad rectangles with number net text, the artwork image. The first die also carries the STEP of the whole assembly. No copper.wb_thickness_um, wb_attach_um. A die that isn't on the board also has wb_sits_on and wb_bottom_um.
Wire, W01…wireOne line on the Wirebond layer. Its tube is in the assembly STEPwb_from, wb_to, wb_type, wb_net, wb_seq, maybe wb_to_kind
Type library, WBLIBtype_libraryNothingOne wb_type_<name> per wire type that's used

Nets stay on the lands. The die has no copper, so KiCad's ratsnest and DRC see only your board's copper. A die pad's number and net are Fab text, because KiCad clears the number of a pad with no copper when it loads one.

If you move the die in KiCad, the wires stay where they were. KiCad doesn't know they're connected. Load the board back into the app, let the wires follow the die, and export again. The new export replaces the old die, wires and type library.

There are two KiCad conventions in this repo. The app writes the one above. examples/kicad/example.kicad_pcb was generated from example.wb.svg by make_kicad.py and uses an older one: die pads on F.Cu, one footprint per land, and loop profiles as wb_profile_<i>_… board properties. The app reads both. See examples/kicad/README.md for the older one.

6Where the export is going partly built

This section answers four questions: which 3D format to write, how the 3D gets into KiCad, what a KiCad library package should hold, and whether the wirebond.svg itself goes into the KiCad file. The first two are decided and built: one STEP, embedded in the board. The other two are still proposals.

The KiCad behaviour described here was checked with kicad-cli 10.0 on 15 November 2026. What was checked and what is still to test is listed at the end of the section.

Why one WRL per wire didn't work

  • KiCad's STEP export leaves the wires and the die out. It prints Cannot use VRML models when exporting to non-mesh formats and writes the board without them. The board's STEP is what goes to mechanical CAD, so mechanical CAD never sees the bonding.
  • The workaround is opt-in. With a STEP file of the same name next to each WRL, KiCad uses the STEP, but only with --subst-models or the matching box ticked in the export dialog. Most exports would still miss the wires.
  • It's a lot of loose files. The 74-wire example writes 75 files into 3d/, linked by ${KIPRJMOD}/3d/… paths. Copy the board without that folder and the 3D is gone.
  • VRML is a picture, not a part. It's a mesh for rendering. Mechanical CAD tools mostly don't import it, and it isn't true geometry to check clearances against.

Ways to do the 3D

OptionLoose filesKiCad 3D viewerBoard STEP exportBoard file alone is enoughNotes

B · WRL plus a STEP twin for each150YesOnly with --subst-modelsNoKeeps today's look, doubles the files, and depends on a checkbox.

Decided and built: D. One STEP holds the die and all the wires, attached to the first die's footprint and embedded in the board. VRML is gone. The zip also has the same STEP as a loose file for anyone who wants it in mechanical CAD directly.

E is not needed. The app is where the bonding is edited: to change anything, change it there and export again, and the new STEP replaces the old one in the board.

Embedding files in a KiCad board

KiCad 9 added embedded files, and the KiCad 10 demos use them for 3D models. A footprint's model path is kicad-embed://name.step. The file's data sits once at the end of the board, and each footprint that uses it lists its name and checksum. The data is zstd-compressed and then base64-encoded.

The browser can write this with no extra library, which was the main doubt. A zstd frame may hold uncompressed ("raw") blocks, which takes a dozen lines to write. KiCad accepts a lowercase SHA-256 of the uncompressed bytes as the checksum, and browsers compute that natively with crypto.subtle. A test board built that way exported to STEP with the embedded model in it. Because the data isn't compressed, the board grows by about 1.33 times the file's size. KiCad keeps the data as it was written when it saves the board, so the board stays that size.

Writing STEP in the browser

The die is easy: a box for the attach layer, a box for the die and thin boxes for the pads. Wires are tubes along a curve. There are several ways to write a tube in STEP:

Wire geometryForAgainst
A chain of short exact cylindersTrue wire diameter for clearance checks. Each piece is three simple faces, so it's the easiest to get right.Small steps at the bends. Roughly 3 kB per segment, so a few MB for the 74-wire example. That's a guess to measure.
One swept B-spline face per wireSmooth, and the smallest file, around 10 kB per wire.The hardest to write correctly: the seam, the end caps and the surface's orientation.
A faceted mesh, as flat facesTrivial maths, the same mesh as today's WRL.Each triangle becomes about 15 STEP entities, so files get very large.
OpenCascade in wbx.pyExact sweeps, joined solids, colours, all from a mature library (CadQuery or build123d).A heavy Python dependency, and not in the browser, so the app couldn't write it.

Built: the chain of cylinders, in app/js/step.js. Each wire's path is thinned to what its own diameter can show, about 12 cylinders a wire, and each cylinder runs a little past a bend so the outside of the bend has no notch. The 74-wire example is 964 solids in 1.9 MB of STEP, which puts 2.5 MB into the board. KiCad renders it in colour and passes it to its board STEP export. B-spline sweeps remain the way to shrink it if that size becomes a problem. Each solid is coloured: wires by their type, the die grey and the pads white. The die artwork is what was lost. The old WRL drew it as a mosaic of coloured rectangles. In STEP that would be thousands of small faces, so the STEP leaves it out. The footprint keeps it as an image.

What goes into the KiCad board

The structure stays as it is: ordinary footprints with hidden wb_ fields. Three things are added: the embedded STEP, which is built, and a KiCad group and the wirebond.svg as an embedded snapshot, which are still proposals. UUIDs and formatting are left out.

(kicad_pcb …
1  (footprint "wbx_die"  ; D1, wb_kind die, as today
    …
    (embedded_files (file (name "wbx_bond.step")
      (type model) (checksum "…")))
    (model "kicad-embed://wbx_bond.step"))
2  (footprint "wbx_wire" …)  ; W01…, no model
3  (footprint "wbx_types"  ; WBLIB
    (property "wb_type_STD25" "…")
    (property "wb_snapshot" "sha256:…"))
4  (group "wirebond D1"
    (members <D1> <W01> … <WBLIB>))
5  (embedded_files
    (file (name "wbx_bond.step") (type model)
      (data |KLUv…|) (checksum "<sha256>"))
6    (file (name "bonding.wb.svg") (type other)
      (data |KLUv…|) (checksum "<sha256>"))))
  1. The die footprint carries the assembly STEP, so the die and every wire appear in 3D wherever the die sits.
  2. Wire footprints keep their fields and their line on the Wirebond layer, and drop their models. They're still what a KiCad user selects, inspects and edits.
  3. wb_snapshot is a hash of the die, wire and type fields and positions as they were exported. Reading the board back, the app compares it with what it finds.
  4. A KiCad group holds the die, wires and type library together. Moving the group moves the 3D and the wire lines with the die, which fixes the most common way today's export goes stale. Moving the die out of the group still breaks the wires, as it does now.
  5. The STEP's data lives once, at the end of the board.
  6. The wirebond.svg rides along as a file of type other. KiCad keeps it through a save. Board Setup lists it, and it can be saved out from there.

Should the wirebond.svg itself go in?

Where it goesSurvives a KiCad saveReadable in a git diffCan go staleNotes
Nowhere: the fields are the record (today)YesYesNoThe app rebuilds a .wb.svg from the board. Anything the fields can't hold is lost.
As text in a propertyYesBadlyYesA 70 kB string in one field, shown in KiCad's properties dialog, with every edit a one-line diff. Not recommended.
As an embedded fileYesNoYes, detectablyChecked: KiCad keeps it, compresses it and rewrites its checksum when it saves.
As a file next to the boardNot touchedYesYesOpens in a browser and diffs well in git. It's one more file to keep with the board, like the .kicad_pro.

Proposal: the fields stay the source of truth, and the embedded file is a snapshot. When the board is read back and wb_snapshot still matches the fields, nobody has changed the bonding in KiCad, and the app opens the embedded .wb.svg whole. When it doesn't match, the fields win. The app rebuilds from them, takes only what the fields can't hold from the snapshot, and says what it did.

Today the app can rebuild almost everything from the fields, so the snapshot matters most for what the format grows into, such as placement tolerance, fiducial roles, notes and new wire data, and for a .wb.svg someone else wrote. The zip also has the same .wb.svg as a loose file, for git and for the bonding house.

Packages for a KiCad library

KiCad treats a part as three things that agree with each other: a schematic symbol, a footprint and a 3D model. The die kit already has the first two, and has a WRL as its model. The proposal makes every KiCad export a complete part, with a STEP for the model and a .wb.svg as the record.

Checked with kicad-cli 10.0

· STEP export drops VRML models, with the message quoted above.

· --subst-models swaps in a same-named STEP.

· An embedded model lives at board level, and the footprint refers to it by name and checksum.

· A raw-block zstd frame with a lowercase SHA-256 loads. Uppercase, or a wrong checksum, gives "Checksum error" and the model is dropped.

· A file of type other survives kicad-cli pcb upgrade, which loads and saves the board.

· The app's STEP loads, renders in colour, follows a turned die, and passes through to the board STEP export.

· The 74-wire example: 964 solids, 1.9 MB of STEP, 2.5 MB in the board. A save keeps the raw blocks as they are.

· A die kit footprint with its STEP embedded survives kicad-cli fp upgrade.

· Exporting an exported board again replaces its STEP, rather than adding a second.

Still to test

· Moving a group in KiCad keeps the die, its model and the wire lines together.

· The board STEP opens in FreeCAD, or another MCAD tool, with the wires in it.

· The bonded module's symbol and footprint pass KiCad's footprint and symbol checks.

7Recipes

From a KiCad board and a GDS to a bonded board the usual way in
  1. Draw the padring in KiCad.One footprint, with numbered SMD pads on F.Cu for the lands and one big pad for the paddle. Give the lands nets if you know them (level 1).
  2. Drop the board, its .kicad_pro and .kicad_sch.Check the app picked the padring footprint as the substrate. If not, choose it under Substrate.
  3. Drop the die's GDS.Check the pad count and pin 1 under Die. If the pad layer is wrong, pick another or change the size limits.
  4. Pick a regime.The Regimes tab compares every way of pairing pads with lands. Hover to preview one, click to use it, then fix single wires by hand. Wires you edit are pinned.
  5. Read the Checks tab.Set the rules to your bonding house's numbers under DRC rules if you have them.
  6. Put it in KiCad → Assembled board.Unzip it and open the _bonded.kicad_pcb. The wires are on the Wirebond layer and in the 3D viewer.
No GDS yet a die from a footprint
  1. Make a footprint for the die.One numbered pad per bond pad, at its centre, with the pad opening as its size. Put the net name in the pin function. Draw the die outline as a rectangle on F.Fab.
  2. Drop it with the padring.Drop both footprints together and the smaller becomes the die. Or drop it on the Die box.
  3. Work as usual, then swap in the GDS later.Drop the GDS on the Die box to replace the footprint. Check the pad numbering matches what you used, then run the regimes again.
Edit a bonded board and bring it back the round trip
  1. Open the exported board in KiCad and work on it.Route, move other parts, add things. You can also edit a wire's wb_to or wb_type in its properties.
  2. Drop the saved board back into the app.The die comes back from its wb_kind = die footprint, the types from WBLIB, and the wires under the From the board regime.
  3. Export again.The old die, wire and type footprints are removed and new ones written. Nothing else on the board changes.
Send it to a bonding house files they can use
  1. Export the bond diagram (.wb.svg) and the bondlist (.csv).The diagram is the drawing a person reviews. The bondlist is the table.
  2. Check it.python tools/wbx.py check board.wb.svg lists errors, warnings and notes, and returns non-zero on errors. wbx.py place says how far the die can sit off its nominal place before a check fails.
  3. Make bonder files.python tools/wbx.py export board.wb.svg board writes the 3D DXF, the bondlist and the fiducials.
  4. Send a PDF and the table too.No bonding house expects .wb.svg yet. Print the diagram from a browser.

8Round trips

10Limits

  • OASIS isn't read. Export the die as GDS. Reading .gz needs a browser with DecompressionStream, which current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari have.
  • The 3D has no die artwork. The STEP shows the die as a plain block with its pads. The footprint keeps the artwork as an image.
  • A turned die loses its artwork in the board too, because KiCad doesn't turn a footprint's image.
  • The STEP is stored uncompressed. The board holds it as raw zstd blocks, about 2.5 MB for the 74-wire example, and KiCad keeps it that way when it saves.
  • The STEP follows the die, not the wires. Move the die footprint in KiCad and the whole assembly's 3D moves with it; move a wire footprint and its tube stays. Export again from the app to bring them back together.
  • Die thickness and attach height are assumed. They're written into the board and the STEP, so set them under Placement before exporting.
  • Pad finding is checked only on the wafer.space template die, in the 1x1 and 1x0p5 slots. Check the pad count and numbering on other dies.
  • wbx.py reads one die at 0°. The app writes turned and stacked die correctly, but wbx.py can't check them yet.
  • Nothing here has been bonded. The formats and checks haven't been through a real bonder or a bonding house's software.
